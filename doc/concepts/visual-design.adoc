= Visual Design: The Register

:toclevels: 3
:toc-title: Table of Contents

:source-highlighter: highlight.js

xref:../README.adoc[Audit Notary documentation] » Concepts

Audit Notary is a form-based application: every document is edited in a form generated from its category's schema
(xref:web-app.adoc#_forms_from_schemas[Web App, Forms from Schemas]). This page records how it looks and why. It keeps
the structure of `pm-web-app` — the shell, the four page templates, the closed vocabulary of components, the tag
grammar and the column engine (xref:web-app.adoc[Web App]) — and replaces its visual language with one of its own,
grounded in what the application is: a notary's office for security documents.

The implementation is `web-app/src/styles/tokens.css` and the components of `web-app/src/lib/components/`; the
working rules for whoever changes them are the project skill `.claude/skills/audit-notary-design/SKILL.md`.

== Why an Own Visual Language

`pm-web-app` is a control room for running work: an indigo-violet accent on a warm off-white, a dark palette close to
GitHub's, cards with border and shadow, 36 pixels as the largest type. That language suits a dashboard of processes.
Audit Notary's pages are records: an assessment a team fills in over two hours, a release an approver signs, an
incident report a supervisory authority may read. The person needs to see the record itself, not a control panel
around it.

== The Idea: The Form Reads Like the Record It Will Become

Every document is an entry in a register, every save a signed line, every release a seal. Five decisions follow from
it.

=== The Sheet and Its Margin

A document is one sheet of paper on a desk (`Sheet`): white, nearly square corners, a soft shadow beneath it, on a
cool paper-grey ground. The record takes the left of the sheet — the title at a display size in the serif, the
sections from the nesting of the schema, the fields with labels above and full visible boundaries. A released
document is the same sheet with its values typeset, so reading and writing share one layout.

To the right runs the *register margin*, divided from the record by the double rule of a ledger's margin. It holds
what the record does not: who works on it, the signed history (every save is a signed commit, so every line names who
did what and when), the release and its tag. Its top, the *status*, holds what must stay in reach — the save state and
"Save for everyone" — and on a narrow sheet it stands as a band above the record, while the rest of the margin follows
the record. The sheet measures its own width, never the viewport: in a column it is as narrow as the column.

=== The Seal

The one bold element. A SAMM assessment shows its 15 practices of the five business functions as a scalloped rosette
of 30 lobes (`MaturitySeal`), one per stream, each as long as its stream score on the 0 to 3 scale; the target of a
practice is a dashed arc across its two lobes, the hub states the overall score. It echoes the scalloped seal of the
mark (xref:brand.adoc#_the_mark[Name and Brand, The Mark]). It is navigation too: every practice is a link with its
full accessible name, and the table beside it states the same figures.

Released, a scalloped ring of gold surrounds the rosette with the version on a tab: the seal is pressed into the
sheet. That press is the application's one orchestrated motion; under reduced motion the seal simply stands. The seal
gold appears nowhere else as a colour of state; a released document's state label wears a small gold seal, and a
release line in the history a gold dot.

A document without a rosette, a note of the generic documents lens, wears the same ring around its version
(`ReleaseSeal`): the scalloped ring of gold, the word "Released" and the version in the serif, pressed in once when the
release is made, in the same motion. It appears only after the release; before it, the release view shows the facts of
the document and the changes. After the release the record folds, so that the seal is the one bold thing on the page,
and the margin tells what was done; where the release cannot be taken, it tells why and promises nothing. The notary stands beside either seal, because a finished release is a calm surface; the release of an incident has none, because its deadlines weigh on the page.

=== The Daybook

The start page (*Due*) is kept like a register's daybook: groups *Overdue*, *Today*, *This week*, *Later*, one day
after the other with the day's numeral in the serif in the gutter. An overdue entry carries a box with its reason,
because it needs the person; every other entry says its day quietly. The grouping follows the clock, so "today" turns
into "overdue" without a reload.

=== Columns as a File Drawer

The lenses with a chain of levels (SAMM: scope › assessment › practice) keep the column engine of `pm-web-app`
(`columnWindow.ts`). The list columns lie on the desk and stay in view while the sheet beside them scrolls; the object
column holds the sheet, raised above them. The engine admits a larger minimum for an object that needs room — the
practice sheet keeps 820 pixels and the window hides a list column instead — and the hidden levels stand in the strip,
by name or by mark.

=== Workshop Speed

A SAMM practice is answered in a meeting. Each question shows the model's answers as one row of choices, a small gauge
on each saying how much of the answer it is (`AnswerScale`); the number keys 1 to 4 answer the question in focus, J
and K move between questions, and a hint shows the key on the question in focus (switchable, hidden on touch screens).
A change is kept as the person's draft within seconds ("saved for you"); "Save for everyone" writes one signed commit.
A conflict shows each differing question with both answers; a save by another assessor refreshes what nobody changed
here and says who saved. The answer texts are those of the model's answer sets
(link:https://github.com/owaspsamm/core/tree/v2.2.0/model/answer_sets[OWASP SAMM core v2.2.0, answer sets]), served as
data with the model, never written into the catalogue.

The status colours (attention, danger, success, running) keep one meaning each; colour never carries meaning alone —
every state has its word, every chart its labels or table.

Every text on every ground it is rendered on — the plain grounds, the rail, the tinted grounds of badges, notices,
the diff and the chosen answer — is checked in `tokens.test.ts` in both themes against WCAG 2.1 success criterion 1.4.3
(link:https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG21/#contrast-minimum[4.5:1]), every state graphic and the field boundary against 1.4.11
(link:https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG21/#non-text-contrast[3:1]). The five chart colours are slots 1 to 5 of the reference
palette of the data-visualisation method used here, validated against both sheet colours for colour-vision deficiency
and normal-vision separation; in the light theme four of them stay below 3:1 against white, so every chart states its
values in text or a table as well.

== Type

* *Inter* for the interface (taken over from `pm-web-app`), with tabular figures.
* *Source Serif 4* for the record: sheet titles at the display size of 40 pixels, section heads, question texts, the
  daybook's numerals, the figures of a score. The variable roman with its optical-size axis, so a title gets the
  display cut and a question the text cut; self-hosted under `web-app/static/fonts/` with its licence, the SIL Open
  Font License 1.1 (link:https://github.com/adobe-fonts/source-serif/blob/4.005R/LICENSE.md[`LICENSE.md` of
  `adobe-fonts/source-serif`, release 4.005R]).
* No labels in capitals, no strings of facts joined by dots: facts are a list of terms and values.
* Monospace only for what a person compares or copies: release tags, pseudonyms.

== What Stays from pm-web-app

The shell (rail, top bar with the one breadcrumb, bottom bar on the phone, command palette; the top bar shows no
connection state, by the owner's decision — the event stream reconnects by itself instead), the templates, the closed
vocabulary of components with no raw control in a view, the tag grammar (a box means "needs you", a dot means "for
information"), one primary act per screen, a disabled control that stays focusable and says why, the column window,
the focus moved to a new object and announced, light, dark and system themes.

== The Forms

The form of a document is generated from its schema (`SchemaForm`, xref:../specification/web-app.adoc#AUN-WEB-4[AUN-WEB-4])
and stays in the register's voice: labels above full field boundaries in the interface face, the word *required* after
the label of a mandatory field, help in a muted line under it. Sections come from the nesting of the schema and are divided
by a hairline and a serif heading, never by a box. A list is a table; its rows are edited in a dialog over a copy, so a
cancelled row changes nothing, and on a narrow column each row stands as a block of its columns. Read, the same form
shows every value typeset in the serif under its label: a date localised, an option by its text, a person by name.
What a reference stands for is said in words: a dot for information (*retired*, *not yet bound*, *former member* with
team and role, never a name), a box for what needs the person (*not found*, *unknown person*, a row with an error).
The AsciiDoc preview sits under its source behind a hairline margin; it is the server's rendering, cut to an allow-list
and drawn with the app's own elements.

A dialog (`Dialog`) is never taller than the room under its top margin (the window less that margin and one step of space);
what does not fit scrolls inside it, so a long row editor never runs past the lower edge, on a phone as on a desk.

== The Documents Lens

The documents of the categories without a lens of their own are one more chain of columns: category, documents, document
(`TriagePage`, xref:../specification/rest-api.adoc#_the_generic_documents_lens[REST API Register, The Generic Documents
Lens]). The list column holds title, state, version and the last change of each document, with a box only for a pending
release request; the sheet is the form of the category's schema, read and written in the layout of the record. Three
things are the lens's own. The steps of the workflow stand above the form as one bar: the one primary act is *Save for
everyone* while something waits to be saved, and the step otherwise, because a step acts on the saved document; with
unsaved changes the bar asks to save first. A conflict is one panel above the form that lists every field someone else
changed, with the two versions side by side and the decision per field; nothing is saved until every field is decided.
An earlier state of the document reads in the same layout, typeset, under a notice that names it and a link back; the
choice of the earlier state is in the margin. The neutral kind marks are the category (the stack of sheets of the lens
icon) and the document (the page with a folded corner).

== The Catalogs Lens

The catalogs of the directory (teams, roles, services, systems, external parties) are one more chain of columns:
catalog, entries, entry (`TriagePage`,
xref:../specification/rest-api.adoc#_directory_addresses[REST API Register, The Addresses]). The first column is the fixed
list of the five catalogs, each with the sentence that says what it holds, and needs no request; the list of entries
holds the name and the id of each, a dot for a retired one (nobody has to act) and the action *New*, which says why it
cannot be taken for a person without the right. The sheet of an entry is the form of the catalog's schema in the layout
of the record, with the one primary act *Save*; the id is read, never edited, once the entry exists. Retiring is a
destructive act: it stands in the margin, asks first and says that what refers to the entry keeps its reference and shows
it as retired. A retired entry and any entry for a person without the right read in the same layout, typeset; that the
entry can only be read is said once, in the status of the sheet, and no notice repeats it. The status and the reason of
a disabled *Save* are never the same sentence: the status says where the entry stands ("Everything is saved."), the
reason says why the act is out of reach ("Nothing changed since the last save."; for a new entry "Fill in the form
first.", because nothing was saved before).

The sheet of a service adds one section below the form: the protection needs stored on the service beside the effective
ones, per dimension, with the service or dependency that decides each, and the dependencies as a table whose rows are
indented by their depth and carry the way they were reached, their needs and the reason they count or do not (a dot, never
a colour alone). A cycle of dependencies is a notice above the form, never a refusal. The neutral kind mark of an entry is
the cube (`KindMark` kind `entry`); a field that names several catalogs (`dependsOn`) lists their entries grouped by
catalog under the group's name (`Select` groups its options). On a phone the rows of both tables stand as blocks of
labelled lines; the reason a dependency does not count is a sentence that wraps beside its dot (the dot stays beside
the first line), the three dimensions of a dependency stand as two aligned columns that never break between the term
and the level, and every link of a stacked row is a target of 44 px (its padding, not a box).

== The SAMM Completion

Four things are added to the SAMM lens (UC-SAMM-1 to UC-SAMM-5); each reads in the register's words and adds no component
to the closed vocabulary beyond the three named here.

*A new scope and a new assessment* are sheets in the object column at `/samm/new` and `/samm/{scope}/new`, built like
the form of a new document: the fields stand in one narrow column of the record, the status holds the save state and the one
act (*Create scope*, *Start assessment*), the margin says in a line what is not stored yet. Nothing is stored before that
act, and leaving the page asks (`LeaveGuard`, the one dialog every new object shares). The subject of a scope is a group of
radio buttons (team, service, the whole organisation) with the picker of the catalog below it; the assessors of an
assessment are the first person, named by the session and needing no pick, and as many `PersonPicker` rows as the person
adds, each with a quiet act to remove it. An assessment that is open already is an attention notice naming it, with its
link, and the act says why it cannot be taken.

*The targets* are a table of the practices by business function, in the register's words: a table of facts read row by row
(`GridTable`), whose cells are a `Select` (the maturity from 0 to 3 in steps of a half, or no target) and a date field. The
controls carry their label for assistive technology only (`labelHidden`), because the head of the column says what they
are; on a narrow column each row stands as a block of labelled lines. In the scope sheet the table is part of the record and
is saved like the record: the status holds the save state and *Save for everyone*, and a conflict stands above the table, a
block per practice with both entries and the two ways to decide. A scope has no draft, so the sentence of the status says
"Changed. Not saved for everyone yet." instead of "Keeping it for you".

*The evidence* of a practice is a section after its streams, a `DataTable` of links (title, address, what it belongs to)
with a dialog that edits one link over a copy; removing a link asks first, and the limit of fifty is a sentence where the act
would be. An address is a link only when it is an absolute `https` address. The comment of an answer is an `AsciiDocField`
with the server's preview; a released assessment shows it formatted, as the server renders it.

*The frozen assessment* is the released one. Its sheets are read-only and say so once, in the status; the way on is the
one act *Start a new assessment*, a primary link in the head of the assessment and in the status of its practice. A
comparison with the previous release that the server refuses (`MODEL_VERSIONS_DIFFER`) is an information notice in the place of
the changes, naming both assessments and both versions; the scores stay readable.

== The Reports

The lens *Reports* is the launcher template (`DashboardPage`): the dashboard `/reports` and the four reports are sections on
the desk, divided by hairlines, never cards and never a sheet in a sheet. The top bar's breadcrumb is the way back; no page
draws a second one. The dashboard says each register in figures set in the serif (a number above its term), the ones that
need the person in the colour of their state and always with the term in words, then the report's link. A report holds no
act that writes; its controls only choose what is read (a scope, two assessments, an order, a day or a tag).

*Two charts are added to the closed vocabulary.* `SeriesChart` draws several lines on one fixed scale from 0 to 3: the
scopes of the trend (the series 1 to 5 in the order of the scope list) or the five business functions of one scope (the
series 1 to 5 in model order). A series is a slot of the validated series colours and, beside the colour, a marker of its
own shape (circle, square, diamond, triangle, cross), so that no line depends on a colour alone. The end of every line
carries its value as a direct label; labels that would touch are moved apart (down, and up again where the last would leave the figure), and a label that
no longer stands at the end of its line is tied to it by a leader in the line's colour that runs sideways as well as down, so that two leaders never read as one stroke. A chart of four or five lines is taller (300 instead of 220 px), so
that its labels move less. Under forced colours the lines lose their colours: each slot then keeps a dash of its own beside its marker. A legend names every series, and the page
repeats every figure as a table under the chart. A sixth scope has no line: the table has it, and a line says so.
`Dumbbell` shows the same measure at two moments: a ring at the earlier score, a filled dot at the later one, a bar between
them, and the two scores and the change in words beside it. The ring and the dot differ in form, not only in place. The scale of figures stays on a phone, above the rows and over the same stretch as their tracks, with the scores in a column of fixed width. A
practice and its streams stand in a disclosure under their business function.

*The threat evaluation* is one table component (`ThreatEvaluationTable`), used by the report and by the lens *Threat
models*: a review that is overdue or due within 30 days is a box (`ReasonBadge`) with its words, one in date or never released
a dot. *The risk register as of a day or tag* takes a day or the name of a tag in one field with the tags of the register
beside it; what the server refuses is said beside the field and the value stays in it.

== The Threat Models

The lens *Threat models* is a chain of columns (`TriagePage`, xref:../specification/rest-api.adoc#_threat_model_requests[REST API Register, Threat Model Requests]):
the services, the service with its models, the model as a sheet. The first column is the evaluation table of the report
(`ThreatEvaluationTable`) in the order of the server, and a service without a model is offered the act *New threat model*
there, in the table row, for a person who may create one. The address of a new model is the recipe wizard (below).

*The sheet of a model* reads in the layout of the record: the review, the diagram, the threats, the countermeasures and the data
of the model, each a section divided by space and a hairline (no card, no sheet in a sheet), the status and the margin as every
document has them. The review is the one act of the sheet that is the person's own: *Start the review* sets the review date to
today and says why it is spent when it already is; the date of the last release and the date the next review is due stand
as terms above it, an overdue or soon due review as a box with its words, one in date a dot.

*Two editors are added to the closed vocabulary*, both over the form kit's `DataTable` with a dialog over a copy of the row,
so that giving up changes nothing and removing asks first. `ThreatTable` changes the status of a threat in its row (a select
with a hidden label that names the threat) and the rest in the dialog; a threat that is open, treated by no countermeasure and
not transferred carries the box *Untreated*, the only box of the row besides the status dot; a threat that was transferred
names the risk it became as a link and cannot be changed by the sheet; removing a threat says that its countermeasures go with
it. `CountermeasureEditor` lists every countermeasure with the threat it treats, its status and its issue ids as `Chip`s (they
are compared and copied); one whose threat is not in the model carries the box *Unknown threat*. The acts of the rows are named
by the id of the row (`Remove T-03`), because the page holds two tables. A list is saved as a whole and a conflict is one
panel entry per list, told by the ids and names of its entries.

*The recipe wizard* (`/threat-models/{service}/new`) is a sheet in the last column of the lens, like the sheets of a new scope and
a new assessment, and not a page of its own: the person keeps the service beside them. Without a recipe the sheet offers the
recipes, each with what it covers, the number of its steps and, for one the person has started, where they stopped (a dot, the
state is information) and the act *Continue*; with one it reads the steps of the recipe. The new piece of the closed vocabulary
is `Stepper`: the steps as a numbered list, the current one marked by `aria-current="step"` and an ink underline, the steps
already reached by a check and as acts that go back to them (they are named "<step> (done)" for a screen reader), the steps not
yet reached as plain text, so that the way forward is the sheet's own act and never a jump. On a phone only the count ("Step 2 of
5") and the current step stay. A step is the heading of the step, its guidance (the recipe's AsciiDoc, rendered by the server,
set off by a hairline at its start and never in a box), and the form of the part of the model it fills: the form kit for an object
or a table of objects, and the table of the threats and the editor of the countermeasures where the step fills those two lists. The
step's heading names its only part, so the form is `untitled`: the section title or the table caption it would draw a second
time stays for a screen reader. A
change of step moves the focus to its heading and is announced politely. The last page, *Review and create*, lists every step with
what it holds and an act to change it, asks for the owner and the review date (prefilled with today), and carries the one primary
act, *Create threat model*. The status of the sheet holds the save state (*Saved for you at …* once the step is kept, the same
sentence as every draft) with the primary act and *Back*; the margin says that nothing exists for others before the create.

*The diagram* is an image of the generated vector drawing, never inline markup: it is drawn only from a base64 `image/svg+xml`
data URL, with a text alternative made from the names of the components, on its own white ground with a hairline in both themes.
*The release* reads each threat and countermeasure that changed as one change of its own, by its id, with the two states side
by side, and the review date as a date.

== The Incident Reports

The lens *Incidents* is a chain of columns (`TriagePage`, xref:../specification/rest-api.adoc#_incident_report_requests[REST API Register, Incident Report Requests]):
the years, the incidents of a year, the incident as a sheet. The first column lists the years the person may read, each with
its count, the incidents not yet released, a box when an incident has a missed deadline, and how long the year is kept; the
second lists the incidents newest awareness first, each row with its state, its severity, the status of each of its stages (a
check, an empty ring, a dash, and always the word for a screen reader), a box for the deadlines that were missed and the next
open one. *New incident* is a link in the primary look at the top of the column, offered only for the current year.

*The sheet of an incident* stands in the order of the work: the head, the steps of the workflow (*Submit for review*, disabled
with the stages still open as its reason), the deadline clock, the stages, the report, and in the margin the save state, the
release, the earlier states and the signed history. The title is a form of its own at the top of the triage tab, since the title is
no stage.

*Two components are added to the closed vocabulary.*

* `StageTabs`: the stages of a staged document as a tab list (the ARIA pattern: one tab in the tab order, the arrow keys, `Home` and
  `End` move the focus, `Enter` or `Space` chooses). Each tab carries the name of the stage in the serif, the status in words
  (a dot with *Open*, *Completed* or *Does not apply*), the marker *Signed off before an earlier stage was reopened* when it applies,
  and a mark that needs the person (an undecided conflict) as a box. A stage that does not apply stays a tab, greyed, and says
  under which condition it applies (or that the question is not answered yet), so that its kept data can be read. The selected
  tab has the ink edge under it (on a phone beside it), never colour alone. On a phone the tabs stand as full-width rows of 44 px.
* `DeadlineClock`: the notification deadlines as a list of rows: the name of the rule and its legal basis, the state in words
  (*Open*, *Met*, *Missed*), the due time in the serif with tabular numerals, and the sentence of the time (due in, overdue by, sent,
  sent late). It is sober on purpose: no mascot, no seal gold, no icon of its own, no motion, no gauge. A deadline that needs the
  person (missed and never sent, or open and past its time) is a box, every other state a dot and its word, so a missed deadline sent
  late stays visible and is not an alarm. The clock is told against the page's own time while the incident is worked on, and
  against the time of the release once it is frozen, which the page says.

*A stage* reads as a panel under the tabs: its name, who signed it off, the guidance for the stage, the notices of the page, the
form of its part (the form kit; a completed stage is read-only), and at its end the act of the stage. *Complete stage* acts on the
saved part, so with changes only the person has it asks to save first; a refusal says what is missing, each field as a way to it, and
which stage comes first. The stage that asks for the confirmation of the identity says so in a line beside the act.

*The notices* are a table over the form: every notice that was sent with its time, recipient and channel and the deadline it
meets, and under it the form of the part (*Record a notice*), where each notice has its own section. *The report* is read on the page,
section by section, only when the person asks, with the note that it is a preview from the saved state and written in English.

*The release of an incident* is `IncidentReleaseView` on `ReleaseFlow` and adds no component. It is a sheet in the order of what the
approver decides: the facts of the incident (the seal after the release, never before), the deadlines that are still a duty
(`DeadlineClock`, with the sentence that a released incident is frozen and a link to the tab *Notifications*; after the release the
sentence that the clock has stopped and the same rows told against the time of the release), then the record by stage: one group for
the title and one for each stage that applies, each field worded as its form words it, the sign-off of a stage as the row *Stage
signed off* with the person's name. The act reads *Release*; the margin counts the open stages and deadlines and promises nothing
where the act is disabled. A release the server refuses because a stage is not completed is a notice that nothing was released, with
a link of the secondary look for each stage (`ReleaseFlow` takes `explain`, the refusal only one document type knows). The deadlines
do not block the release.

*A new incident* is a sheet in the last column (`NewIncidentSheet`, `/incidents/{year}/new`): the title and the time the organisation
became aware as two forms of the form kit, the primary act *Create incident* with the reason it waits (the title), and the leave guard
of the unsaved. A person without the right, or a year that is not the current one, gets a sentence and the way back to the year.

== The ISMS Documents

The documents of the ISMS (the policies, the scope of the ISMS, the audit reports, the management reviews;
xref:../use-cases/07-isms.adoc#UC-ISMS-1[UC-ISMS-1]) are the generic documents lens on the addresses of the lens *ISMS*
(`IsmsView`, xref:../specification/rest-api.adoc#_decided_isms[REST API Register, points 77 to 84]): three columns,
the types, the documents of a type, the document. They add no component but one that is shared, and they are built from what
the other lenses built. The sheet, the new document and the release are `DocumentSheet`, `NewDocumentSheet` and
`DocumentReleaseView` over `ReleaseFlow`, which take the addresses, the facts and a block of the margin from the lens.

*The first column* is the types: the serif name of the type, how many documents it holds and a dot with the word *in review* when some
wait, and below a hairline the way into the risk register. *The second column* is the documents of one type, each row the title, the
state, the last release (*Last release v3, 20 May 2026*: a document is released more than once, so the number of the version is how
many releases it has had) and, for a document in work, the working version and who changed it.

*The review date* is a fact under the title of the sheet (*Review due*), in the ink of the text: the date alone when it is far away,
the date and the days when it is within 30 days or overdue. It has no colour, no box and no seal gold; an overdue review that needs a
person is the Due lens's item, not a mark on the sheet.

*The comparison of two releases* is a page, `/isms/documents/<id>/compare`: a dashboard page with the breadcrumb of the lens. Two
pickers over the releases and the act *Show the changes* stand at the top, then the pairs of neighbouring releases when none is
chosen, or the two sides (version, date, who released it, a link to read it) and the record of the changes. The act is a link in the
primary look that says what is missing while it cannot be taken.

*One component is shared, not new.* `DiffRecord` is the record of changes that the release page already drew: one folding section per
property of the document, each change with the words of its field, the earlier value struck out and the later value marked as added;
the release page and the comparison draw it the same way, and what a value is drawn as (`DiffValue`) is handed in.

== The Pages of the Directory

Three pages belong to the lens *Catalogs* and *Settings* but are not a chain of columns, because each is a task, not an
object: the persons (`/directory/persons`), the Backstage import (`/directory/import`) and the own entry
(`/settings/profile`). Each is a single column on the desk. The persons page is a dashboard page: a search, then the
table of the persons (name, team and role in words, the state as a dot — *bound to a login*, *not yet bound* — and, with
the right, the act *Erase*), then the entry of several persons at once as rows divided by hairlines, each a name and its
memberships, with the one primary act at the end. The table is `GridTable`: on a narrow column each row stands as a block
of labelled lines, so nothing scrolls sideways. Erasing asks first in a dialog that names the person by the name the table
shows and says that it cannot be undone; a directory kept in the organisation's own source is one notice that says nothing
was changed. Without the right the page says so once, in a notice, and shows neither act. The notices of the page stand in
the width of its sections, never across the whole column; the *Erase* of a row is a quiet act (no box) because the dialog
that follows is the weight of the decision; the name of a row keeps room, so a name is not broken between its words. The
words differ on purpose: *retire* (catalog entries) keeps the entry and what refers to it, *erase* (a person) removes the
entry for good, and the German *stilllegen* and *löschen* keep that difference.

The import is a wizard of three steps named above the column (choose documents, check, result), with the current step
marked by `aria-current` and by weight, not by colour. Choosing is a `FilePicker` and a field to paste into; the chosen
documents are a list with their size and a box on the one the server found invalid. The check writes nothing and says so
in a notice, and reads in the future: the figures and lists are those *to create* and *to update*, never *created* before
anything is written; it shows what would be created and updated as tables, what stays unchanged as a figure and what was left out
as a table of the entity, the sentence of its reason and its detail. The import is the one primary act and is not offered
when nothing would change; the result repeats the tables with the entries linked. What the introduction says once (nothing
is fetched from Backstage) is not said again in the notice of the check, which says that nothing is written. An entity
reference of the left-out table is read whole: its column keeps room, so an id is not broken in the middle.

The own entry is the form of the first login in the register's layout: the name, the memberships as rows of a team and a
role, the information on data processing. When the confirmation is due, the question *Is this still right?* stands above
the form with its one act; while there are unsaved changes that act says why it cannot be taken ("Save your changes first."), because the
question already says that saving confirms as well, and the reason does not say it again. Elsewhere in the shell the due confirmation is an information notice with a link, on every view but this one.

The first login shows the candidates an administrator prepared as one group of radio buttons by name, team and role, with
the choice to create a new entry as its last option; a refusal of the choice is an attention notice above the group, and the
mascot goes while a refusal or an error is shown. The way from the list of the catalogs to the persons and the import is two
rows of the same list (`RowLink`) under a hairline, not text links.

== The Access Pages

Three pages stand outside the shell, because there is no session to show a shell for: `/login` (the form of local test
accounts; with the identity provider it only starts the login), `/signed-out` and `/login-problem`
(xref:../specification/rest-api.adoc#_addresses_outside_the_shell[REST API Register, Addresses Outside the Shell]). Each
is the flow template (`WizardPage`) in the `GateFrame`: the name above one column on the desk, no rail, no top bar, no
sheet. The notice of local test accounts stands above the column of each page and, with local users, above every view of
the shell (the same `AccessNotices`); it is an attention notice, because the installation is not meant for production. A
user logged in without any right sees a notice and, in place of every view on a repository, an empty state with a lock;
the shell and the settings stay. The signed-out page is a calm surface and shows the notary; the login problem and the
empty state without access are refusals and show none.

The confirmation of a release, a transition or a save is one dialog, "Confirm your identity", owned by the step-up
handler (`src/lib/stepup/`); its button names the domain act ("Release", "Submit for review"), never the method.

== Accessibility

* WCAG 2.1 AA in both themes, checked by `tokens.test.ts` and by axe-core on every view in `web-e2e`; no violation of
  impact `serious` or `critical`.
* Every control is a native element of the closed vocabulary: radio buttons for answers, a switch for the key hints,
  `<details>` for folded content. The keyboard reaches everything; the number keys are an addition, never the only way.
* The seal's practices are links whose accessible name starts with the abbreviation drawn beside them and goes on
  with the practice's name, score and target, so the visible label is part of the name (WCAG 2.1 success criterion
  2.5.3, link:https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG21/#label-in-name[Label in Name]).
* Motion answers an act or marks the one release moment; under `prefers-reduced-motion` it is off. In forced-colours
  mode the seal and the meters fall back to system colours.
* The phone keeps every act: the save band stays above the record, the answers take two columns, the margin follows
  the record.

== The Mascot

As the brand rules have it (xref:brand.adoc#_the_mascot[Name and Brand, The Mascot]): only on calm surfaces — the empty
Due list, the signed-out page and a finished release, where the notary stands beside the seal. Never in an incident, a deadline or a
warning.
